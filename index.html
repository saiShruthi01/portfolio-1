📊 About Me

I started out building things — NLP pipelines, retrieval systems, agents that reason through multi-step tasks — because I wanted to see AI actually ship and work at scale. Along the way, a quieter question kept surfacing underneath the technical one: not just how to build a system that understands language, but why people communicate, form communities, and construct identity the way they do, especially online and across cultures.

That question is what's pulling me toward research now. I'm especially drawn to computational social science and Cultural Technology — using multilingual NLP, multimodal AI, and agentic systems to study things like moral emotion in online discourse, fan communities, and digital identity, in the spirit of interdisciplinary programs like KAIST's Graduate School of Culture Technology. I'm learning Korean along the way, partly out of practical necessity, and partly because I think you understand a research community a little better once you can read what it writes in its own language.

Day to day, I still work like an engineer: Python, PyTorch and TensorFlow, LLMs from GPT to XLM-R, LangGraph agents, RAG pipelines over vector databases, and dashboards in Streamlit or Plotly to make the results legible to someone who isn't reading the code. I'm just increasingly interested in pointing all of it at questions about people, culture, and media rather than only at business metrics.

💼 Experience

Software Engineer · Oct 2024 – Present · Hyderabad, India

Most of my time here goes into making machines make sense of language across languages. I built an NLP pipeline that processed 60,000+ multilingual commuter-feedback entries for Dubai's transport authority — language detection, translation, sentiment analysis, and named entity recognition with sentence transformers — and turned the output into a geospatial dashboard that urban planners could actually use for decisions, not just a report to file away.

I also spend a lot of time in LangGraph, building an agentic AI system that reasons through multi-step tasks, runs tools in parallel, and holds memory across a conversation across multiple LLM providers — plus a RAG pipeline on a Milvus vector database for semantic search over unstructured knowledge, and 50+ REST APIs to hold it all together.
